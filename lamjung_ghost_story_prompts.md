# "THE PATH ABOVE THE DEAD" — A Lamjung Family Story
### (Short film screenplay — Nepali dialogue with English subtitles)

> **Note on names:** I've used "Kale" as a placeholder name for the boy (your father as a child) since you didn't share his real name. Swap it everywhere with the real name if you want, or keep it as-is if you'd rather keep it slightly fictionalized for privacy.

> **Note on the Nepali dialogue:** I've written natural, simple village-Nepali lines as best I can. Exact phrasing and local dialect matter a lot for authenticity — please have a Nepali speaker (yourself, your father, a relative) review and adjust the lines before you generate. I want this to sound like your village, not like a translation.

## 1. STORY CONCEPT

**Where it's from:** Your village in Lamjung district — your father's home sits at the top of the village, inside the jungle, and above the house is the village's burial ground.

**The real memory:** When your father was around 10–11, he'd play with friends and have to cross the burial ground path to get home. One evening, coming back alone, a man was sitting there and called out to him. Every villager always warned the kids: if someone calls you from that place, don't answer, don't look — just go. Your father ignored the warning, and ran as fast as he could, all the way home.

**Format:** A short film with real dialogue and performance — not a narrator talking over footage. Around 12–15 minutes, built scene by scene like a movie.

---

## 2. CHARACTERS

- **Kale** — the boy, 10–11 years old (your father as a child)
- **Buwa/Aama** (Father/Mother) — waiting at home
- **Village elder (Grandfather figure)** — delivers "the warning" earlier in the film, giving weight to the ending
- **Friends** — 2–3 other village kids, present in the opening scenes only
- **The Man** — sits at the burial ground; minimal dialogue, deliberately unclear who/what he is

## 3. FULL SCREENPLAY

### SCENE 1 — INT/EXT. VILLAGE — DAY
*Wide shots of the village, terraced fields, Kale's house at the top edge near the jungle. No dialogue — pure visual establishing, natural village ambience only.*

### SCENE 2 — EXT. VILLAGE OPEN YARD — LATE AFTERNOON
*Kale and 2–3 friends playing — running, a simple game like dandi biyo or tag.*

**FRIEND 1** (laughing, shouting):

**FRIEND 2** (looking at the sky):

> *Subtitle: "It's getting dark, time to go home."*

*The friends split off toward their own homes. Kale is left to take the long path alone — the one that passes the burial ground.*

**FRIEND 1** (walking away, calling back):
> "म यता जान्छु, तँ त्यता जा। ध्यान देऊ है!"
> *Subtitle: "I'll go this way, you go that way. Be careful, okay!"*

**KALE** (waving, a little nervous):
> "हुन्छ, भोलि भेटौंला।"
> *Subtitle: "Okay, see you tomorrow."*

*(Optional flashback insert here or earlier in the film — see Scene 3B below — showing the elder's warning, so the audience already knows the rule before Kale faces it.)*

### SCENE 3B — FLASHBACK/INTERCUT. VILLAGE ELDER'S WARNING (can be placed earlier in the film, e.g. right after Scene 1)
*An elder speaking to a group of village children, including Kale, sometime before this evening — establishing the village rule.*

**ELDER** (serious tone):
> "सुन है, ती चिहानको बाटोमा कसैले नाम बोलाए भने कहिल्यै नहेर्नू, नबोल्नू। जे भए पनि हिँडिरहनू।"
> *Subtitle: "Listen carefully — if someone calls your name near that burial path, never look, never answer. No matter what happens, just keep walking."*

**KALE** (younger, half-serious):
> "किन बा? को बोलाउँछ र त्यहाँ?"
> *Subtitle: "Why, Grandfather? Who would call from there?"*

**ELDER** (not answering directly, just firm):
> "भनेको कुरा सुन्, बहस नगर्।"
> *Subtitle: "Just listen to what I say. Don't argue."*

### SCENE 4 — EXT. FOREST PATH — DUSK (near dark)
*Kale walking alone. The path narrows, trees close in. He's clearly trying to move quickly, glancing around. The burial ground comes into view ahead — simple stone markers among the trees.*

*No dialogue — just his breathing, footsteps, the forest sounds thinning out.*

### SCENE 5 — EXT. BURIAL GROUND EDGE — DUSK
*Kale sees a MAN sitting still at the edge of the burial ground, facing the path. Kale slows, uncertain.*

**THE MAN** (calm, almost gentle — this is what makes it unsettling):
> "ए काले... यता आइज न।"
> *Subtitle: "Hey, Kale... come here."*

*Kale freezes. Wide eyes. The Elder's warning from Scene 3B flashes in his mind (quick visual insert of the elder's face/words, 1 second, no new dialogue needed — reuse Scene 3B footage as a flash-cut).*

*Kale does not answer. Does not look directly at the man's face. He turns and runs.*

### SCENE 6 — EXT. VILLAGE PATH — NIGHT (the run)
*Kale sprinting through near-darkness, breathing hard, village lights visible ahead. No dialogue — just breath, footsteps, and building sound design (see Section 5).*

### SCENE 7 — EXT/INT. KALE'S HOUSE GATE — NIGHT
*Kale bursts through the gate. His mother is waiting, alarmed.*

**AAMA** (rushing to him, worried):
> "के भयो काले?! किन यत्रो हतारमा दगुरेको?"
> *Subtitle: "What happened, Kale?! Why are you running like this?"*

**KALE** (breathless, shaken, deciding not to fully explain):
> "केही होइन आमा... म ठीक छु।"
> *Subtitle: "It's nothing, Ama... I'm okay."*

*Kale looks back once at the dark path behind him, then steps inside. Door/gate closes.*

### SCENE 8 — EXT. FOREST PATH / BURIAL GROUND — PRESENT DAY
*An ADULT MAN (Kale, now grown — your father, present day) stands at the base of the same trail in daylight, looking up toward the tree line. Calm, reflective. No dialogue needed — or a single optional closing line:*

**ADULT KALE** (quiet, to himself or to camera):

> *Subtitle: "That path is still there."*

## 5. BACKGROUND SOUND DESIGN

Flow/Veo 3.1 generates some ambient audio automatically from your prompt (footsteps, wind, ambient noise), but layer these on top in an editor for real impact:

**Layer 1 — Base ambience (constant, low, throughout):** rural Nepal evening sounds — distant dogs, insects, wind, occasional cowbell. Search "village ambience night" on YouTube Audio Library or Freesound.org.

**Layer 2 — Scene-specific:**
- Playing scenes: bright laughter, footsteps on dirt, playful shouting.
- Path alone (Shot 4): ambience thins — warmth fades, insects and footsteps dominate.
- Burial ground (Shot 5): near silence except wind. A very low, barely audible drone (60–100Hz) instead of music.
- The run (Shot 6): heavy footsteps, heartbeat layered in, breathing, wind rushing.
- Reaching home (Shot 7): sudden warm drop — indoor ambience, quiet voices, relief audible.

**Layer 3 — Score:** sparse low drone under Shots 4–6 only, rising slightly during the run. Avoid constant horror music under the whole film — silence broken by sudden sound works better and costs nothing.

**Free tools:** YouTube Audio Library, Freesound.org, Audacity (mixing/effects), ElevenLabs free tier if you need any additional processed voice work.

## 7. BUILDING THE FULL 12–15 MINUTE FILM IN FLOW

- **Choose Image first** for each shot above (boy, elder, man, mother, village) to lock a consistent look, then animate image-to-video, rather than generating straight from text each time.
- **8 shots won't fill 15 minutes on their own** — each Veo generation is 4–8 seconds. To reach a real short-film runtime:
  - Use **Extend** on the dialogue-heavy shots (5, 7, 3B) to let each scene breathe — a few seconds of silence/reaction after a line lands is what makes dialogue feel real, not rushed.
  - Add a few extra **silent connective shots** per scene (a hand on a gate, feet on the trail, wind in trees, a mother's worried face waiting) — these are cheap to generate and are exactly what real films use to control pacing.
  - Realistically, a 12–15 minute short film built this way is more like 35–50 total generations once you include reaction shots and Extends — still very achievable, just more than the 8 core story beats.
- **Consistency tip:** reuse the same reference image for Kale, the Elder, the Mother, and the Man across every shot they appear in, rather than re-describing them — this is the single biggest thing that will make it look like one film instead of disconnected clips.
